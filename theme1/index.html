Yes. I move fluidly between shaping product strategy and shipping high-fidelity UI. In early-stage work, that means co-defining scope with founders and PMs — using Jobs to Be Done, opportunity mapping, and rapid prototyping. In mature organisations, it means running design critiques, owning the design system, and translating roadmap goals into polished, production-ready flows. The level I operate at is whatever the team needs most at any given sprint.

AI is now core infrastructure for how I work — not a side tool. I use LLMs for synthesis (user research, competitive analysis, rapid copy iteration), generative tools to accelerate early visual exploration, and I design for AI products: chatbots, copilots, and agentic interfaces. I think carefully about where AI adds genuine value vs. where it creates noise, and I apply that same systems-level thinking when designing AI-driven experiences — ensuring the model's behaviour is legible, controllable, and trustworthy to the user.

I define success metrics before designing, not after. Primary metrics are typically business outcomes — conversion rate, activation, retention, revenue per user. Secondary metrics cover usability — task completion rate, error rate, time-on-task. For AI products I also track trust indicators: override rate, confidence calibration, and re-engagement after AI errors. At Booking.com, this approach translated directly: +16% conversion lift, +1,500 bookings/day.

I treat design systems as products, not style guides. That means a token architecture (primitive → semantic → component), clear governance, contribution workflows, and versioned documentation. I've built systems from scratch and inherited messy ones at scale. Either way, I focus on three things: adoption (engineers and designers actually use it), flexibility (it scales to edge cases without breaking), and velocity (teams ship faster because of it, not despite it). I work natively in Figma and hand off to React/Storybook.

I've designed chatbots, copilots, and agentic interfaces — from the first user message to error recovery and human handoff flows. The hard problems aren't visual: they're epistemic. How does the user understand what the AI can and can't do? What happens when it's wrong? I design explicit affordances for uncertainty, graceful failure states, and progressive disclosure of AI capabilities. I also think carefully about prompt design, feedback loops, and how the UI shapes the user's mental model of the underlying system.

I keep Figma files production-ready: named layers, auto layout, responsive frames, complete component states, and interaction specs. I speak the language of constraints — I know what's expensive to build and design accordingly. I've written React components when it unblocked a sprint and joined code reviews to catch UI drift early. My goal is zero ambiguity at handoff and zero surprises in QA.
